# Moral Turpitude Lookup — Expansion batch 1 (October 12, 2026)

**Scope:** California criminal witness impeachment. Draft research expansion only; do not merge without review. The original database held 25 offense entries; this branch adds 11, making 36.

## New entries and research posture

| Statute | Result in draft | Principal source and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| PC 484(a) theft | Yes | *In re Hallinan* (1954) 43 Cal.2d 243, 247–248 (fraud/theft dishonesty); context is attorney discipline, coupled with *Castro* felony conviction standard. |

| PC 118(a) perjury | Yes | *In re Kristovich* (1976) 18 Cal.3d 468 (attorney discipline); application to *Castro* reflects inherent dishonesty and must not be represented as a directly tested modern §118(a) impeachment holding. |

| HS 11377(a) simple possession | No (elements-based application) | *Castro* distinguishes simple drug possession from sale; not a separate direct holding about every §11377(a) substance. |
| HS 11378 possession for sale | Yes | *Castro* establishes the possession-for-sale principle; *People v. Morales* (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 1176 discusses §11378 felony priors as morally turpitudinous. The discussion is not a standalone statutory-elements adjudication. |
| HS 11379(a) sales/transportation for sale | Yes (parallel-statute reasoning) | *Navarez* directly addressed §11352, not §11379; trafficking alternatives are comparable. This is an explicit analogy, not a direct §11379 published appellate holding. |
| HS 11550(a) under influence | No (elements-based application) | *Castro* distinguishes simple possession from possession for sale; no direct published witness-impeachment holding on §11550(a) was independently verified. | 
| VC 10851(a) unlawful vehicle taking/driving | Conditional research | *People v. Hunt* (1985) 169 Cal.App.3d 668, 674–675 recognizes auto theft as turpitudinous; §10851 also reaches non-theft driving. |

## Follow-up on drug-offense distinction
The user requested a clear split: simple drug possession/use should be **No** (HS 11377(a), HS 11550(a)); possession for sale and trafficking should be **Yes** (HS 11378, HS 11379(a)). These values have been applied to the draft data on October 12, 2026. The basis is *People v. Castro* (1985) 38 Cal.3d 301, 317 for the fundamental distinction, *People v. Navarez* (1985) 169 Cal.App.3d 936 for analogous trafficking, and *People v. Morales* (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 1176 for its discussion of a witness's §11378 felonies. Modern §11379(a) and §11550(a) are not represented as directly resolved by the Castro/Navarez holdings. Check applicable historical statute, case grade, §352 and citation treatment before courtroom use.
