# Moral Turpitude Lookup — Expansion batch 1 (October 12, 2026)

**Scope:** California criminal witness impeachment. Draft research expansion only; do not merge without review. The original database held 25 offense entries; this branch adds 10, making 35.

## New entries and research posture

| Statute | Result in draft | Principal source and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| PC 484(a) theft | Yes | *In re Hallinan* (1954) 43 Cal.2d 243, 247–248 (fraud/theft dishonesty); context is attorney discipline, coupled with *Castro* felony conviction standard. |
| PC 488 petty theft | Yes, moral character only | *In re Hallinan*; *Wheeler* misdemeanor-conduct rules. A misdemeanor conviction document is not automatically admissible. |
| PC 118(a) perjury | Yes | *In re Kristovich* (1976) 18 Cal.3d 468 (attorney discipline); application to *Castro* reflects inherent dishonesty and must not be represented as a directly tested modern §118(a) impeachment holding. |
| HS 11352(a) narcotic trafficking | Yes | *People v. Navarez* (1985) 169 Cal.App.3d 936, 949–950 directly addresses felony impeachment. |
| HS 11359 cannabis possession for sale | Yes (historic holding) | *People v. Standard* (1986) 181 Cal.App.3d 431, 434–436 expressly so holds for historic felony marijuana possession for sale. Modern California cannabis regulation and offense grading are different. |
| HS 11366 maintaining drug premises | Yes | *People v. Vera* (1999) 69 Cal.App.4th 1100, 1103, treats providing premises for illegal use/sales as turpitudinous. |
| HS 11377(a) possession | Conditional research | *Castro* distinguishes simple heroin possession but does not resolve this entire statute. |
| HS 11378 possession for sale | Conditional research | Analogous *Castro* reasoning, but a directly on-point subsection holding is not yet confirmed. |
| HS 11379(a) drug trafficking | Conditional research | Analogy to *Navarez* §11352; modern law distinguishes transporting for sale. |
| VC 10851(a) unlawful vehicle taking/driving | Conditional research | *People v. Hunt* (1985) 169 Cal.App.3d 668, 674–675 recognizes auto theft as turpitudinous; §10851 also reaches non-theft driving. |
