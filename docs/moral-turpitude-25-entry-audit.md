## Audit standards
- Distinguish a **direct California impeachment holding** from an analogy, dicta, a historical-subdivision mapping, or an entirely different legal context.
- Confirm the cited opinion contains the relevant discussion and examine the pinpoint, the statute's version and crime grade, and whether the factual/procedural posture matches the lookup's use.
- Distinguish **opinion content reviewed** from **formal subsequent-history certification**. This audit does NOT provide negative KeyCite/Shepard's history or an exhaustive citing-reference search; no entry may be represented as independently cleared of all later contrary authority.
- 'No' means not necessarily morally turpitudinous *as a conviction under the elements test*, not that the underlying conduct can never be impeaching.
- The 2022 PDF previously furnished by the user is a secondary research index, not dispositive authority.

## Key legal verification findings
1. *People v. Aguilar* (2016) 245 Cal.App.4th 1010, 1017–1019 (available opinion text) expressly resolves §25400(a)(1) against the defendant, with §352 discretion. Verified for the core classification.
2. *People v. Maestas* (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1552 (available opinion text) treats the willful §1320.5 offense as deceitful; misdemeanor failure-to-appear authority is distinguishable.
3. *People v. Feaster* (2002) 102 Cal.App.4th 1084 (available opinion text) expressly holds §246.3 moral turpitude but affirms trial-court exclusion under §352.
4. *People v. Bedolla* (2018) 28 Cal.App.5th 535 (available opinion text) addresses §25850(a) for impeachment in the setting of a juvenile adjudication and discusses the separate probative-value inquiry.
5. *People v. Burton* (2015) 243 Cal.App.4th 129, published portion (available opinion text) directly addresses §273.5 impeachment, distinguishing an immigration-case framework.
6. *People v. Thomas* (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 689 supports different answers for simple assault/battery, battery with serious bodily injury, and aggravated assault.
7. *People v. Pearson* (2013) 56 Cal.4th 393 does NOT by itself supply a direct subdivision-specific impeachment holding for each form of forgery under §470(a). The lookup must not imply otherwise.
