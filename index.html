    Scope & limitations

    Exposure results are a quick misdemeanor jail reference, not a complete sentencing calculation. Priors, enhancements, multiple counts, mandatory minimums, probation conditions, alternative felony punishment, and other statutes may change the result.

    Probation results are a quick-reference aid. Offense-specific statutes, priors, enhancements, victim relationship, and case facts can change eligibility or required terms. Maximum penal fines exclude penalty assessments, restitution, program fees, and other add-ons.

    A § 29805 “not listed” result means only that the entered offense was not found in that statute; other firearm prohibitions may still apply. Statutory data should be verified before use.

    Forensic toxicology

    Blood Alcohol Estimator

    Estimate an earlier blood alcohol concentration or the theoretical maximum BAC from a reported drinking history. Results are ranges based on ANSI/ASB BPR 122 and should not be treated as an exact measurement or expert opinion.

    Alcohol measurements

    BAC / alcohol readings

    Add each available alcohol measurement. Each reading is extrapolated independently to the target time using the ASB 122 elimination-rate range.

    Multiple-reading methodology

    Additional readings provide independent checks on the estimate and can show whether the extrapolated ranges are consistent with one another. Reference Desk does not derive a personalized elimination rate from the readings; ASB 122 says a rate calculated from two or more test results should not replace the standard elimination-rate range.

    Optional consistency check

    Reported drinking

    Enter the reported alcohol consumed to compare its theoretical maximum alcohol concentration with the retrograde estimates. This does not change the retrograde calculations themselves.

    Absorption assumption

    Reference Desk infers whether post-absorptive status can reasonably be assumed from the reported last-drink time. If no last-drink time is provided, the calculation will not assume absorption was complete.
